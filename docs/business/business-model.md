# Business model

**Status:** Hypotheses to validate
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-24
**Sources:** [MVP specification](../sources.md), [planning session](../sources.md)

## Value exchange

| Actor | Value received | Expected contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company | Trusted candidate introductions without recruiter-style upfront cost | Current job, clear terms, and a meaningful referral budget |
| Referrer | Relevant opportunities for their network and a potential financial reward | Selective, high-quality sharing and honest context |
| Candidate | Better-fit opportunities delivered through a trusted relationship | A consented interest or application signal |
| Community | Useful member service and stronger opportunity circulation | Trust, distribution, identity, and quality norms |

## Revenue hypotheses

### Referral commission

The platform keeps a share of a successful referral bonus. The source discussion used approximately 20% as an example. From a EUR 500 bonus, the platform would keep EUR 100 and pass EUR 400 to the referrer.

Potential benefit:

- revenue aligns with a successful hiring outcome;
- companies avoid upfront search fees;
- payment handling can become a platform service.

Unresolved requirements:

- define the billable success event;
- determine whether payout happens at hire or after probation;
- establish legal, tax, invoicing, and default-risk handling;
- prevent or tolerate disintermediation;
- make attribution and disputes operationally manageable.

### Sponsored listings

Companies pay for featured placement, a community digest boost, top placement, or newsletter visibility.

Potential benefit:

- simpler to operate than contingent settlement;
- monetizes access to trusted distribution;
- can be tested before a hire occurs.

Core risk:

- paid prominence may conflict with trust and relevance if quality is not the first gate.

### Community SaaS

A community, university, alumni organization, entrepreneurship center, or fund pays for a bounded platform or member benefit.

Potential benefit:

- predictable revenue and a natural trust boundary;
- community partners can support identity, supply, and distribution.

Core risk:

- the institution may value the service but lack budget or urgency.

### Hybrid model

A flat platform or listing fee combined with a smaller success commission may balance predictable revenue with outcome alignment. It also combines the operational complexity of both models and should not be assumed before simpler tests.

## Referral-bonus floor

The planning session discussed EUR 100 as a minimum bonus to keep the incentive meaningful. This is not yet treated as an accepted product policy because the relevant threshold may vary by role, community, and expected effort.

## V0 position

- No production payment flow is implemented.
- Bonus values are informational.
- Any settlement is manual or represented by a clearly labeled placeholder.
- The V0 objective is to test the behavior and willingness to pay, not payment infrastructure.

## Highest-priority validation questions

1. Who has the strongest pain and budget: company, community, or another operator?
2. Which event is valuable enough to pay for: visibility, qualified interest, interview, hire, or retained hire?
3. What bonus changes referrer behavior without causing indiscriminate sharing?
4. Does payment handling solve enough legal or operational pain to become differentiated value?
5. Can the platform remain involved after introduction without creating unnecessary friction?

## Suggested experiments

- Ask five job posters to choose between a success commission, sponsored listing, and community-funded access using concrete prices.
- Test one manual referral agreement from listing through final outcome to expose operational edge cases.
- Offer one high-quality sponsored placement without building payment infrastructure and measure willingness to commit.
- Interview one decision-maker at a target community about budget, procurement, member value, and ownership.
