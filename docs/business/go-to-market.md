# Go-to-market

**Status:** Proposed beachhead strategy
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-24
**Source:** [planning session](../sources.md)

## GTM thesis

Launch as a useful tool inside one trusted community, not as an open marketplace. Seed enough credible supply to make sharing worthwhile, activate a small number of well-connected referrers personally, and use real referral behavior to earn broader community distribution.

The source discussion identifies CDTM and WHU as candidate communities and names community connectors, program contacts, professors, and Entrepreneurship Center relationships as potential distribution partners. The initial community is still an open product decision.

## Beachhead criteria

Choose the first community using evidence against these criteria:

- founders and companies already share jobs into the network;
- members regularly recommend candidates to one another;
- at least one trusted connector can support distribution;
- the community contains both job supply and potential referrers;
- the team can personally recruit approximately five testers;
- identity and membership can later be verified without heavy custom work.

## Initial audiences

### Supply side

- alumni founders and startup operators;
- Entrepreneurship Center or venture-program contacts;
- community members with an active hiring need;
- professors or program staff connected to hiring companies.

### Referral side

- highly connected community members;
- program or community connectors;
- alumni who frequently forward roles through email or WhatsApp;
- members with credible domain-specific candidate networks.

## Launch sequence

### 1. Curate supply manually

Start with five to ten credible, current jobs. Confirm company, role, source, referral terms, and contact before activation. A small high-quality catalog is preferable to automated but unreliable supply.

### 2. Recruit a closed test cohort

Invite approximately five target referrers personally through the channels they already use, initially email or WhatsApp. Explain the community context and ask each person to find one role they would genuinely share.

### 3. Close the recipient loop

Observe whether recipients trust the link, understand the role, and know what to do next. Capture a minimal interest signal without forcing a profile or unnecessary personal data.

### 4. Add a community connector

Use early evidence to approach one trusted connector, professor, program contact, or Entrepreneurship Center relationship. Ask for a bounded distribution test rather than a broad endorsement.

### 5. Validate the payer

Run direct conversations with job posters and community operators using concrete revenue options from the [business-model chapter](business-model.md). Do not infer willingness to pay from usage alone.

## Initial channels

| Channel | Role in V0 | Evidence to capture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal email | Recruit job posters and testers | Reply and participation rate |
| WhatsApp | Activate referrers and enable sharing | Completed share actions |
| Community connectors | Borrow trust and reach a bounded cohort | Introductions and activated members |
| Professors and program staff | Reach companies and relevant alumni | Qualified job supply |
| Entrepreneurship Centers | Combine company supply with community distribution | Willingness to run a pilot |

## Proposed activation loop

1. A credible job enters through a known community relationship.
2. An operator verifies and publishes it.
3. A connected member shares it with one suitable candidate.
4. The candidate engages with the opportunity.
5. The team returns the outcome and learning to the job poster and community partner.
6. Demonstrated value attracts the next job and the next trusted referrer.

## Guardrails

- Do not optimize raw invitation or share volume at the cost of referral quality.
- Do not use paid prominence to bypass listing-quality standards.
- Do not claim successful attribution or payout before the process exists.
- Do not expand to another community until the team understands why the first cohort did or did not engage.

## First GTM experiments

1. Compare CDTM and WHU against the beachhead criteria using named connectors, available jobs, and reachable testers.
2. Send a concierge invitation to five referrers and measure qualified share rate and time to share.
3. Ask three connectors whether they would distribute a curated digest or controlled link to a small cohort.
4. Recruit five job posters and test whether email forwarding is easier than manual submission.
5. Present concrete pricing choices after the value has been demonstrated and record objections verbatim.

## Decisions still required

- First community and named owner for the relationship.
- Minimum supply required before inviting referrers.
- Exact invitation message and distribution channel.
- Definition of a qualified share and recipient engagement.
- Trigger for expanding beyond the first cohort.
